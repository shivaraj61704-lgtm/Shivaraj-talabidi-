Important formulas for Board, KCET & JEE revision

1️⃣ Relations & Functions

Composition: (f ∘ g)(x) = f(g(x))

Identity function: f(x) = x

Inverse function: f⁻¹(f(x)) = x

One-one function: f(x₁) = f(x₂) ⇒ x₁ = x₂

Onto function: Range = Codomain

Number of functions from A to B: n(B)n(A)

Number of one-one functions: n(B)! / [n(B) − n(A)]!

2️⃣ Inverse Trigonometric Functions

sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2

tan⁻¹x + cot⁻¹x = π/2

sec⁻¹x + cosec⁻¹x = π/2

tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x+y)/(1−xy)]

tan⁻¹x − tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x−y)/(1+xy)]

sin⁻¹x + sin⁻¹y = sin⁻¹[x√(1−y²)+y√(1−x²)]

3️⃣ Matrices

Matrix multiplication: (AB)ij = Σ aikbkj

(AB)T = BTAT

(A + B)T = AT + BT

A A⁻¹ = A⁻¹ A = I

2 × 2 determinant:

|a b|
|c d| = ad − bc

A⁻¹ = adj(A) / |A|

Matrix A is invertible if |A| ≠ 0.

4️⃣ Determinants

|a b|
|c d| = ad − bc

For a 3 × 3 determinant: expand along any row or column.

|kA| = kn|A| for an n × n matrix.

If two rows or columns are identical, determinant = 0.

Interchanging two rows/columns changes the sign of determinant.

Determinant of identity matrix = 1.

5️⃣ Continuity & Differentiability

f(x) is continuous at x = a if:

lim x→a f(x) = f(a)

Left hand limit = Right hand limit = f(a)

Differentiability ⇒ Continuity

Continuity ⇏ Differentiability

6️⃣ Differentiation

d/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹

d/dx(sin x) = cos x

d/dx(cos x) = −sin x

d/dx(tan x) = sec²x

d/dx(cot x) = −cosec²x

d/dx(sec x) = sec x tan x

d/dx(cosec x) = −cosec x cot x

d/dx(eˣ) = eˣ

d/dx(aˣ) = aˣ log a

d/dx(log x) = 1/x

Product rule:

(uv)' = u'v + uv'

Quotient rule:

(u/v)' = (vu' − uv') / v²

Chain rule:

dy/dx = (dy/du)(du/dx)

7️⃣ Applications of Derivatives

Increasing function: f'(x) > 0

Decreasing function: f'(x) < 0

Critical point: f'(x) = 0

Local maximum: f'(a)=0 and f''(a)<0

Local minimum: f'(a)=0 and f''(a)>0

Slope of tangent = dy/dx

Slope of normal = −1/(dy/dx)

∫1/x dx = log|x| + C

∫cosec²x dx = −cot x + C

∫cosec x cot x dx = −cosec x + C

Integration by parts:

∫u dv = uv − ∫v du

∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx = F(b) − F(a)

📚 Important Definitions with Examples

⚡ PHYSICS — 2nd PUC

1. Electric Charge

Definition: Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter responsible for electric forces.

Example: An electron has charge −1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

2. Coulomb's Law

Definition: The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

Formula: F = (1/4πε₀) × (q₁q₂/r²)

Example: Two charged particles separated by a distance exert an electrostatic force on each other.

3. Electric Field

Definition: Electric field at a point is the force experienced by a unit positive test charge placed at that point.

Formula: E = F/q

Example: A positive charge produces an electric field directed away from it.

4. Electric Potential

Definition: Electric potential at a point is the work done in bringing a unit positive test charge from infinity to that point.

Formula: V = W/q

Example: The potential near a positive point charge is positive.

5. Electric Dipole

Definition: An electric dipole is a pair of equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.

Example: A system containing +q and −q separated by distance 2a forms an electric dipole.

6. Electric Dipole Moment

Definition: Electric dipole moment is the product of the magnitude of either charge and the separation between the charges.

Formula: p = q × 2a

SI Unit: C m

7. Electric Flux

Definition: Electric flux is a measure of the number of electric field lines passing normally through a surface.

Formula: Φ = EA cosθ

Example: Maximum flux occurs when the electric field is perpendicular to the surface.

8. Gauss's Law

Definition: The total electric flux through a closed surface is equal to 1/ε₀ times the net charge enclosed by the surface.

Formula: Φ = qenclosed/ε₀

9. Capacitance

Definition: Capacitance of a conductor is the ratio of charge stored on it to its potential.

Formula: C = Q/V

SI Unit: Farad (F)

10. Dielectric

Definition: A dielectric is an insulating material that becomes polarized when placed in an electric field.

Example: Glass, mica and plastic are commonly used as dielectric materials.

11. Electric Current

Definition: Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.

Formula: I = Q/t

SI Unit: Ampere (A)

12. Drift Velocity

Definition: Drift velocity is the average velocity acquired by free electrons in a conductor due to an applied electric field.

Example: Electrons in a copper wire acquire a small drift velocity when a potential difference is applied.

13. Resistance

Definition: Resistance is the opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current.

Formula: R = V/I

SI Unit: Ohm (Ω)

14. Resistivity

Definition: Resistivity is the resistance of a material of unit length and unit cross-sectional area.

Formula: ρ = RA/l

SI Unit: Ω m

15. Kirchhoff's Current Law

Definition: The algebraic sum of currents entering a junction is equal to the algebraic sum of currents leaving the junction.

Example: At a circuit junction, current entering must equal current leaving.

16. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law

Definition: The algebraic sum of potential differences around any closed circuit is zero.

17. Magnetic Field

Definition: The region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where a magnetic force can be experienced is called a magnetic field.

Example: A current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field around it.

18. Magnetic Flux

Definition: Magnetic flux through a surface is the product of magnetic field, area and cosine of the angle between them.

Formula: Φ = BA cosθ

SI Unit: Weber (Wb)

19. Electromagnetic Induction

Definition: The phenomenon of production of induced emf in a circuit due to a change in magnetic flux linked with the circuit is called electromagnetic induction.

Example: Moving a magnet inside a coil produces an induced current.

20. Lenz's Law

Definition: The direction of induced current is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it.

21. Alternating Current

Definition: An electric current whose magnitude and direction change periodically with time is called alternating current.

Example: Household AC supply is an example of alternating current.

22. Electromagnetic Wave

Definition: Electromagnetic waves are waves consisting of oscillating electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.

Example: Radio waves, visible light and X-rays are electromagnetic waves.

23. Photoelectric Effect

Definition: The phenomenon of emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation of suitable frequency falls on it is called the photoelectric effect.

Example: UV light can cause electrons to be emitted from certain metal surfaces.

24. Work Function

Definition: Work function is the minimum energy required to remove an electron from the surface of a metal.

Symbol: φ

25. de Broglie Wavelength

Definition: The wavelength associated with a moving particle is called its de Broglie wavelength.

Formula: λ = h/p

26. Nuclear Fission

Definition: Nuclear fission is the process in which a heavy nucleus splits into two or more lighter nuclei with the release of energy.

Example: Fission of uranium-235.

27. Nuclear Fusion

Definition: Nuclear fusion is the process in which two light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus with the release of energy.

Example: Fusion reactions occur in the Sun.

28. Semiconductor

Definition: A semiconductor is a material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator.

Example: Silicon and germanium.

29. Intrinsic Semiconductor

Definition: A pure semiconductor without any intentional impurity is called an intrinsic semiconductor.

30. Extrinsic Semiconductor

Definition: A semiconductor whose conductivity is increased by adding a suitable impurity is called an extrinsic semiconductor.

Example: Silicon doped with phosphorus forms an n-type semiconductor.

Important derivations for Board, KCET & JEE revision

⚡ 1. Electric Charges & Fields

1. Electric Field on the Axial Line of a Dipole

Consider an electric dipole having charges +q and −q separated by distance 2a.

Dipole moment:

p = 2aq

Let P be a point on the axial line at distance r from the centre.

Electric field due to +q:

E₁ = (1/4πε₀) q/(r−a)²

Electric field due to −q:

E₂ = (1/4πε₀) q/(r+a)²

Net electric field:

E = E₁ − E₂

Therefore:

E = (1/4πε₀) 2pr/(r²−a²)²

For r ≫ a:

E = (1/4πε₀)(2p/r³)

2. Electric Field on the Equatorial Line of a Dipole

At a point P on the perpendicular bisector of the dipole:

E = (1/4πε₀) p/(r²+a²)3/2

For r ≫ a:

E = (1/4πε₀)(p/r³)

The direction is opposite to the direction of the dipole moment.

3. Gauss's Law

The total electric flux through a closed surface is:

Φ = ∮ E · dA

According to Gauss's law:

∮ E · dA = qenclosed/ε₀

🔋 2. Electrostatic Potential & Capacitance

4. Capacitance of a Parallel Plate Capacitor

Consider two parallel plates of area A separated by distance d.

Surface charge density:

σ = Q/A

Electric field between the plates:

E = σ/ε₀

Potential difference:

V = Ed = Qd/(ε₀A)

Since C = Q/V:

C = ε₀A/d

5. Energy Stored in a Capacitor

Small work done in charging the capacitor:

dW = Vdq

Since V = q/C:

dW = q/C dq

Integrating from 0 to Q:

W = Q²/(2C)

Therefore:

W = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/(2C)

🔌 3. Current Electricity

6. Relation Between Current and Drift Velocity

Let n be the number of free electrons per unit volume.

Volume of conductor swept in time t:

Volume = Avdt

Number of electrons:

N = nAvdt

Total charge:

Q = neAvdt

Therefore current:

I = Q/t
I = neAvd

7. Ohm's Law from Drift Velocity

Drift velocity:

vd = eEτ/m

Since E = V/L:

vd = eVτ/(mL)

Using I = neAvd:

I = ne²AτV/(mL)

Therefore:

V = IR

where:

R = mL/(ne²Aτ)

🧲 4. Moving Charges & Magnetism

8. Magnetic Field Due to a Long Straight Current-Carrying Wire

From Biot-Savart law:

dB = (μ₀/4π) I dl sinθ/r²

For a long straight conductor, integration gives:

B = μ₀I/(2πr)

9. Magnetic Field at the Centre of a Circular Current Loop

For a circular loop of radius R:

dB = (μ₀/4π) I dl/R²

Integrating around the complete circle:

B = μ₀I/(4πR²) × 2πR

Therefore:

B = μ₀I/(2R)

For N turns:

B = μ₀NI/(2R)

10. Force Between Two Parallel Current-Carrying Conductors

Magnetic field produced by the first wire:

B₁ = μ₀I₁/(2πd)

Force on length L of the second wire:

F = B₁I₂L

Hence:

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd)

11. Radius of Circular Path of Charged Particle

Magnetic force provides centripetal force:

qvB = mv²/r

Therefore:

r = mv/(qB)

Angular frequency:

ω = qB/m

🔄 5. Electromagnetic Induction

12. Faraday's Law

Magnetic flux through a coil:

Φ = BA cosθ

Induced emf is equal to the negative rate of change of magnetic flux:

ε = −dΦ/dt

For N turns:

ε = −N(dΦ/dt)

The negative sign represents Lenz's law.

13. Motional EMF

Consider a conductor of length l moving with velocity v perpendicular to a magnetic field B.

Magnetic force on charge q:

F = qvB

At equilibrium:

qE = qvB

Therefore:

E = vB

Potential difference:

ε = El

Hence:

ε = Blv

🔌 6. Alternating Current

14. RMS Value of AC

Instantaneous current:

i = I₀ sinωt

RMS current is defined as:

Irms = √(average of i²)

Since the average value of sin²ωt is 1/2:

Irms = I₀/√2

Similarly:

Vrms = V₀/√2

15. Impedance of Series LCR Circuit

Inductive reactance:

XL = ωL

Capacitive reactance:

XC = 1/(ωC)

Net reactance:

X = XL − XC

Therefore impedance:

Z = √[R² + (XL−XC)²]

16. Resonance Frequency

At resonance:

XL = XC
ωL = 1/(ωC)

Therefore:

ω₀ = 1/√LC

Hence:

f₀ = 1/(2π√LC)

🔭 7. Ray Optics

17. Lens Formula

Using the geometry of refraction through a thin lens and the paraxial approximation, the relation between object distance u, image distance v and focal length f is:

1/f = 1/v − 1/u

18. Lens Maker's Formula

For a thin lens in air, applying refraction at both spherical surfaces gives:

1/f = (μ−1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)

where μ is the refractive index of the lens material.

19. Mirror Formula

Using the geometry of a spherical mirror and the paraxial approximation:

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Magnification:

m = −v/u

🌊 8. Wave Optics

20. Young's Double-Slit Experiment

Let slit separation be d and screen distance be D.

Path difference at a point P:

Δ = d sinθ

For small angles:

sinθ ≈ y/D

Therefore:

Δ = dy/D

For bright fringes:

Δ = nλ

yₙ = nλD/d

β = λD/d

21. Single-Slit Diffraction

For minima:

a sinθ = nλ

where n = 1, 2, 3, ...

💡 9. Dual Nature of Radiation & Matter

22. Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

Energy of incident photon:

E = hν

Part of this energy is used as work function φ.

The remaining energy becomes maximum kinetic energy:

Kmax = hν − φ

Since Kmax = eV₀:

eV₀ = hν − φ

23. de Broglie Wavelength

λ = h/p

λ = h/(mv)

For an electron accelerated through potential V:

λ = h/√(2meV)

☢️ 11. Nuclei

26. Radioactive Decay Law

🧪 Chemistry — Important Definitions with Examples

⚗️ SOLUTIONS

1. Solution

Definition: A homogeneous mixture of two or more components is called a solution.

Example: Salt dissolved in water.

2. Solute

Definition: The component of a solution that is present in smaller amount and gets dissolved is called the solute.

Example: Salt in salt water.

3. Solvent

Definition: The component of a solution that dissolves the solute is called the solvent.

Example: Water in salt water.

4. Molarity

Definition: Molarity is the number of moles of solute present in one litre of solution.

Formula: M = moles of solute / volume of solution in litres

Example: A 1 M NaCl solution contains 1 mole of NaCl per litre of solution.

5. Molality

Definition: Molality is the number of moles of solute present in one kilogram of solvent.

Formula: m = moles of solute / mass of solvent in kg

6. Mole Fraction

Definition: Mole fraction is the ratio of the number of moles of one component to the total number of moles of all components.

Formula: XA = nA / (nA + nB)

🔋 ELECTROCHEMISTRY

7. Electrochemistry

Definition: The branch of chemistry that deals with the relationship between chemical reactions and electrical energy is called electrochemistry.

Example: A Daniell cell converts chemical energy into electrical energy.

8. Electrolyte

Definition: A substance that conducts electricity in aqueous solution or molten state due to the presence of ions is called an electrolyte.

Example: NaCl solution.

9. Strong Electrolyte

Definition: An electrolyte that completely ionizes in solution is called a strong electrolyte.

Example: HCl, NaOH and NaCl.

10. Weak Electrolyte

Definition: An electrolyte that partially ionizes in solution is called a weak electrolyte.

Example: CH3COOH.

11. Electrode

Definition: A conductor through which electric current enters or leaves an electrolyte is called an electrode.

Example: Copper electrode in a copper sulphate solution.

12. Oxidation

Definition: Oxidation is the loss of electrons by a species.

Example: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻

13. Reduction

Definition: Reduction is the gain of electrons by a species.

Example: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu

14. Anode

Definition: The electrode at which oxidation takes place is called the anode.

15. Cathode

Definition: The electrode at which reduction takes place is called the cathode.

⚡ CHEMICAL KINETICS

16. Chemical Kinetics

Definition: The branch of chemistry that deals with the rate of chemical reactions and the factors affecting it is called chemical kinetics.

17. Rate of Reaction

Definition: The change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time is called the rate of reaction.

Example: The concentration of a reactant decreases as a reaction proceeds.

18. Order of Reaction

Definition: The sum of the powers of concentration terms in the experimentally determined rate equation is called the order of reaction.

Example: Rate = k[A]²[B] has overall order 3.

19. Molecularity

Definition: The number of reacting species taking part in an elementary reaction step is called its molecularity.

Example: A reaction involving two reacting molecules has molecularity two.

20. Activation Energy

Definition: The minimum energy that reacting molecules must possess for an effective collision is called activation energy.

Example: A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

🔥 SURFACE CHEMISTRY

21. Adsorption

Definition: The accumulation of particles of a substance on the surface of another substance is called adsorption.

Example: Activated charcoal adsorbs gases.

22. Absorption

Definition: The uniform distribution of one substance throughout the bulk of another substance is called absorption.

Example: Water absorbed by a sponge.

23. Catalyst

Definition: A substance that changes the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed permanently in the reaction is called a catalyst.

Example: MnO₂ catalyses the decomposition of KClO₃.

24. Colloid

Definition: A heterogeneous system in which particles of one substance are dispersed in another substance with particle size generally between true solutions and suspensions is called a colloid.

Example: Milk.

🧲 d- AND f-BLOCK ELEMENTS

25. Transition Element

Definition: An element whose atom or one of its stable ions has an incomplete d-subshell is called a transition element.

Example: Iron (Fe).

26. Lanthanides

Definition: The elements in which the 4f orbitals are progressively filled are called lanthanides.

Example: Cerium (Ce).

27. Actinides

Definition: The elements in which the 5f orbitals are progressively filled are called actinides.

Example: Uranium (U).

🧪 COORDINATION COMPOUNDS

28. Coordination Compound

Definition: A compound containing a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands is called a coordination compound.

Example: [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺.

29. Ligand

Definition: An ion or molecule that donates a pair of electrons to a central metal atom or ion is called a ligand.

Example: NH₃ acts as a ligand.

30. Coordination Number

Definition: The number of donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal ion is called the coordination number.

Example: In [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, the coordination number is 6.

🌡️ THERMODYNAMICS

31. System

Definition: The part of the universe selected for studying a thermodynamic process is called the system.

Example: Gas enclosed in a cylinder.

32. Surroundings

Definition: Everything outside the system that can interact with it is called the surroundings.

33. Enthalpy

Definition: Enthalpy is a thermodynamic quantity representing the heat content of a system at constant pressure.

Example: Heat released during a combustion reaction is related to its enthalpy change.

34. Entropy

Definition: Entropy is a measure of the randomness or disorder of a system.

Example: Gases generally have greater entropy than liquids.

35. Gibbs Energy

Definition: Gibbs energy is a thermodynamic quantity used to determine the spontaneity of a process at constant temperature and pressure.

Formula: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS

⚖️ EQUILIBRIUM

36. Chemical Equilibrium

Definition: A state of a reversible reaction in which the forward and backward reaction rates are equal is called chemical equilibrium.

Example: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃.

37. Dynamic Equilibrium

Definition: An equilibrium in which the forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates is called dynamic equilibrium.

38. Acid

Definition: According to the Brønsted-Lowry concept, an acid is a substance that donates a proton (H⁺).

Example: HCl donates H⁺ to water.

39. Base

Definition: According to the Brønsted-Lowry concept, a base is a substance that accepts a proton (H⁺).

Example: NH₃ accepts H⁺ from water.

🧬 ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

40. Homologous Series

Definition: A series of organic compounds having the same functional group and general formula, with successive members differing by –CH₂–, is called a homologous series.

Example: CH₄, C₂H₆, C₃H₈, C₄H₁₀.

41. Functional Group

Definition: An atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic chemical properties of an organic compound is called a functional group.

Example: –OH is the functional group of alcohols.

42. Isomerism

Definition: The phenomenon in which compounds have the same molecular formula but different structures or spatial arrangements is called isomerism.

Example: Butane and isobutane have the same molecular formula C₄H₁₀ but different structures.

43. Alcohol

Definition: Organic compounds containing a hydroxyl (–OH) group attached to a saturated carbon atom are called alcohols.

Example: Ethanol, C₂H₅OH.

44. Aldehyde

Definition: Organic compounds containing the –CHO functional group are called aldehydes.

Example: Ethanal, CH₃CHO.

45. Ketone

Definition: Organic compounds containing a carbonyl group bonded to two carbon groups are called ketones.

Example: Propanone, CH₃COCH₃.

46. Carboxylic Acid

Definition: Organic compounds containing the carboxyl (–COOH) group are called carboxylic acids.

Example: Ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH.

47. Amines

Definition: Organic derivatives of ammonia in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups are called amines.

Example: Methylamine, CH₃NH₂.

🧫 BIOMOLECULES

48. Carbohydrates

Definition: Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that produce them on hydrolysis.

Example: Glucose and sucrose.

49. Protein

Definition: Proteins are polymers of α-amino acids linked together by peptide bonds.

Example: Insulin is a protein.

50. Enzyme

Definition: Enzymes are biological catalysts that increase the rate of biochemical reactions.

Example: Amylase helps in the digestion of starch.

51. DNA

Definition: DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a biomolecule that stores and transmits genetic information.

Example: DNA is present in the chromosomes of cells.

52. Vitamins

Definition: Vitamins are organic compounds required in small quantities for normal growth and proper functioning of the body.

Example: Vitamin C is found in citrus fruits.

🧴 POLYMERS

53. Polymer

Definition: A polymer is a high molecular mass substance formed by the repeated linking of small molecules called monomers.

Example: Polyethene is formed from ethene monomers.

54. Monomer

Definition: A small molecule capable of joining repeatedly to form a polymer is called a monomer.

Example: Ethene is the monomer of polyethene.

55. Addition Polymerisation

Definition: Polymerisation in which unsaturated monomers combine without elimination of small molecules is called addition polymerisation.

Example: Ethene → Polyethene.

56. Condensation Polymerisation

Definition: Polymerisation in which monomers combine with elimination of small molecules such as H₂O or HCl is called condensation polymerisation.

Example: Formation of nylon-6,6.

🧪 2nd PUC Chemistry Derivations

Important derivations and mathematical results for Board, KCET & JEE revision

1️⃣ Solutions

1. Elevation in Boiling Point

For a dilute solution, the elevation in boiling point is proportional to the molality of the solution.

ΔTb ∝ m

Introducing the proportionality constant Kb:

ΔTb = Kbm

Therefore:

Kb = ΔTb/m

2. Depression in Freezing Point

The depression in freezing point is proportional to molality.

ΔTf ∝ m

Therefore:

ΔTf = Kfm

3. Osmotic Pressure

For a dilute solution, osmotic pressure follows an equation analogous to the ideal-gas equation.

πV = nRT

Since n/V = C:

π = CRT

where C is the molar concentration.

4. Van't Hoff Factor

i = observed colligative property / calculated colligative property

Hence modified equations become:

ΔTb = iKbm
ΔTf = iKfm
π = iCRT

2️⃣ Electrochemistry

5. Relation Between Gibbs Energy and Cell Potential

Electrical work obtained from a reversible electrochemical cell is:

W = nFE

Maximum useful work is related to Gibbs energy:

ΔG = −W

Therefore:

ΔG = −nFE

Under standard conditions:

ΔG° = −nFE°

6. Nernst Equation

The thermodynamic relation is:

ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q

Using:

ΔG = −nFE

and:

ΔG° = −nFE°

Substituting:

−nFE = −nFE° + RT ln Q

Therefore:

E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q

At 298 K:

E = E° − (0.0591/n) log Q

7. Relation Between Conductivity and Resistance

Resistance of a conductor is:

R = ρl/A

Conductivity is the reciprocal of resistivity:

κ = 1/ρ

Therefore:

κ = l/(RA)

8. Molar Conductivity

Molar conductivity is conductivity multiplied by the volume containing one mole of electrolyte.

Λm = κ × 1000/C

where C is concentration in mol L⁻¹.

3️⃣ Chemical Kinetics

9. First-Order Integrated Rate Equation

For a first-order reaction:

Rate = −d[A]/dt = k[A]

Rearranging:

d[A]/[A] = −kdt

Integrating between initial concentration [A]₀ and concentration [A]:

ln([A]₀/[A]) = kt

Converting to common logarithm:

k = (2.303/t) log([A]₀/[A])

If initial concentration is a and amount reacted is x:

k = (2.303/t) log[a/(a−x)]

10. Half-Life of First-Order Reaction

At half-life:

[A] = [A]₀/2

Using the first-order equation:

k = (2.303/t1/2) log 2

Since log 2 = 0.3010:

t1/2 = 0.693/k

11. Arrhenius Equation

k = Ae−Ea/RT

Taking logarithm:

ln k = ln A − Ea/(RT)

In common logarithm form:

log k = log A − Ea/(2.303RT)

4️⃣ Thermodynamics

12. Relation Between ΔG, ΔH and ΔS

Gibbs free energy is defined as:

G = H − TS

For a change at constant temperature:

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS

For standard conditions:

ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°

For a spontaneous process:

ΔG < 0

At equilibrium:

ΔG = 0

ρ = mass/volume

Mass of one unit cell:

mass = ZM/NA

For a cubic unit cell:

volume = a³

Therefore:

ρ = ZM/(a³NA)

√3a = 4r

a = 4r/√3

Face-Centred Cubic:
